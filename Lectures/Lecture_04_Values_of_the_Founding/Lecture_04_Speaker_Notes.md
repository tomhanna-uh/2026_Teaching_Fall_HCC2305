## Classical liberalism

Stress: **not** modern “liberal vs conservative” party labels. Enlightenment package: limit power, expand who participates, let *individuals* and voluntary associations flourish.

### Coyne restatement
Close paraphrase of Chris Coyne (GMU): the major problem of government is to allow us to live together peacefully in a society of dignified equals. Ties directly to the course arc.

### Fulfillment restatement
**Empowering individuals to pursue their own fulfillment** — individualistic, not collectivist. Not “groups” or “the community’s fulfillment.”

Speaker language you asked for, cleaned:
- This is a progressive / modernistic gloss on “life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness.”
- Happiness here is not cheap thrills or momentary pleasure.
- Closer to finding your own highest purpose and fulfillment (you can mention Maslow / self-actualization as a familiar campus reference without requiring students to know the theory).
- Classical liberalism is individualistic: the person, not the collective, is the unit that gets to aim at fulfillment.

## Spectrum of limits

Walk from most restrained to most expansive. This is the core of the hour.

**Thoreau:** “That government is best which governs not at all” (*Civil Disobedience*, 1849). Pacifism / pure consent edge.

**“Governs least”:** Quote is from *The United States Magazine and Democratic Review*, not Thoreau. In notes only: people often attribute a similar spirit to Thoreau; Thoreau’s actual line is stronger (“not at all”). Don’t put the misattribution on the slide.

**Krauthammer:** civilized society keeps organized violence at the *lowest possible* level needed for agreed functions.

**Constitutional limits:** force only when and how the constitution allows. Buchanan (near-unanimity for constitutional rules vs majority for ordinary politics) is **saved** for the constitutional-politics lecture.

**Social contract table:** people give up some freedom / accept some coercion; government protects life, liberty, property. Hobbes (strong sovereign to escape war of all), Locke (retained rights + right of resistance), Rousseau (general will) — names as different bargains, not full theories.

---

## Hard cases (discussion)

**Militant democracy:** suppressing parties/movements labeled threats to democracy. Can protect democracy or become protection of the established parties (Madison / Publius warning about majoritarian danger).

**Realpolitik:** national interest and results over ideal moral limits on force.

**Discussion prompt:** Where do students place these two — closer to the restrained end, or closer to theocracy / totalitarianism? No single required answer; force reasoning.

**Theocracy / totalitarianism:** Mussolini and Stalin one-liners land the expansive end. Don’t dwell on biography.
