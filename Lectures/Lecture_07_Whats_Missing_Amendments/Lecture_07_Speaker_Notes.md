# Lecture 7 Speaker Notes — What's Missing / Amendments (HCC)

**Date:** September 9, 2026  
**Course:** GOVT 2305 Federal Government

---

## Empowerment

Students should leave able to: (1) name the original bargain’s failure by its own standards, (2) map the main formal renegotiations (Civil War + voting amendments), (3) state why formal amendment is hard and valuable, and (4) hold Spooner’s consent question open while seeing why that makes basic rights *more* ethically urgent.

---

## Narrative spine (video order)

Unfinished contract → breach (slavery as antithesis; excluded groups) → Douglass → amendments as renegotiation → ink needs backup → ERA unfinished + 14th angle → Spooner open → “what else?”

---

## Douglass (tight slide + spoken excerpts)

On slide: title of the 1852 speech, short respect quote, and the “condemn + invite” move.

**Spoken options (from the speech):**
- Respect for the signers as brave men who treated justice and liberty as final, not slavery.
- The Fourth of July is a day of mourning for the slave when measured against the nation’s professed principles.
- The invitation is to *make the ideals real*, not to discard them.

Full text/video you already host: https://tom-hanna.org/political-speeches/what-to-the-slave-is-the-fourth-of-july-fredrick-douglass/

**Graphic proposal:** Public-domain portrait of Frederick Douglass (e.g., National Archives / Library of Congress 1870s photograph). Place in columns beside the quote slide if desired.

## Amendments

Civil War trio as the radical rewrite after the breach. Voting/participation cluster (17, 19, 24, 26) as expanding who gets a formal say. Keep tables thin; speak the “why it mattered.”

17th fits “maybe direct election of Senators” — popular participation and a partial shift away from state-legislature appointment.

---

## 1971 + ERA block

“103 years” = ratification of 14th (1868) to meaningful sex-discrimination equal-protection application (Reed v. Reed 1971 is the usual landmark; you can name it or keep it chronological).

**ERA framing (your notes):**
- Attempted, not ratified → unfinished renegotiation; “almost” is not enough.
- Angle A: possibly unnecessary; possibly a bit degrading to single women out as a class needing special protection when the 14th already covers persons.
- Counter: 14th is not explicit on sex → relies on goodwill of future Supreme Courts.

Hold both without forcing a student conclusion.

---

## Enforcement list

CRA 1964, VRA 1965, Title IX, ADA — “ink needs backup.” You may copy links or short explainers into Canvas; slides stay as the short list.

---

## Outside formal amendment

One slide only. Statutes, judicial interpretation (incorporation, etc.), political practice. Defer substance to Bill of Rights and Courts lectures.

---

## Spooner

Leave **open**. Three steps on the slide. Closing beat: because almost no one gives explicit personal consent, *respecting basic rights* is both an ethical duty and a practical necessity if the system is to claim anything better than naked force. Do not resolve into anarchism or into “consent is irrelevant.”

---

## Discussion close

“What else needs to be done along these same lines — expanding the circle of those protected and those who participate?”  
Let students propose; you can tie forward to later civil-rights / direct-action material without previewing too much.
