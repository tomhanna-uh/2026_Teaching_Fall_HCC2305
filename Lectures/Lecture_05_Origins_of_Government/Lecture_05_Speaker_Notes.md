## Empowerment

Two answers to “where does government come from?”: **Olson** (empirical) and **Locke** (normative rights/legitimacy). Both matter for *why we limit* power later.

---

## Collective action

Ostrom line is the hook. Prisoner’s Dilemma video is for **in-class** show (YouTube link on slide). Emphasize: without enforcement, cooperation collapses; that is a core justification for coercive state capacity — and for worrying about that capacity.

**Free rider:** Hangover image only. Classroom translation: group projects, public goods, “someone else will do it.”

**Security as first public good:** Who provides? Who pays? Sets up both Olson and Locke.

---

## Olson (empirical)

Roving vs stationary bandit (bandits.png). Stationary bandit’s long-term tax interest → incentive to protect production and provide order.

**Do not put on slide — speaker only:** Democracy can be framed as a more inclusive form of stationary banditry (coalition of citizens; leaders cater to a wider electorate). Useful provocation; keep out of the projected text so it does not look like an endorsement of cynicism.

One-line takeaway: order can grow out of self-interested violence. That is descriptive, not a moral blessing.

---

## Locke (normative)

State of nature ≠ Hobbesian war; law of nature; free and equal. Inconveniences: no settled law, no neutral judge, no reliable enforcement.

Contract image → purpose: preservation of property (life, liberty, possessions). Rights pre-exist; government protects, does not create.

**US founding as rare real contract:** Constitution signing image. Consent, natural rights, right to alter or abolish — a rare written instance of the theoretical idea, not proof that every state was born that way.

---

## Two models

Gears / comparison table. Olson = where states can come from. Locke = why we may claim legitimacy and demand limits. Conceptual map image: tools, not full history.
