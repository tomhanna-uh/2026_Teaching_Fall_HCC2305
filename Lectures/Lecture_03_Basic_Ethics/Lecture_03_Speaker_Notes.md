# Speaker Notes — HCC GOVT 2305 Lecture 03: Basic Ethics
**Fall 2026 | August 24**

---

## Goals

1. Empowerment: students can state a personal standard for when force is acceptable and apply it to government.
2. Link Lecture 2 definition (organized coercive physical force + legitimacy) to ethics.
3. Trolley → reciprocity (quotes intact) → formal labels → trusteeship → delegated violence.
4. Leave time for personal-ethics discussion block.
5. Aristotle moved to Lecture 4.

---

## Opening

- Empowerment slide once, pause.
- Bridge: if government *is* coercive force, ethics is the subject, not a side topic.

## Trolley

- Image first; you narrate the classic setup.
- Word slides (Utility / Omission vs Commission / Ends and Means) — you expand.
- Discussion: lever or not; does the ratio change the answer?
- Optional: "Would you defend this to a future grandchild?" (from prior student).

## Golden Rule block

- Title: Reciprocity.
- Read each quote; let attribution sit. Do not rush.
- Confucius: columns layout with `confucius.jpg` beside text.
- Optional portraits to download later (Wikimedia Commons, public domain / free):
  - Buddha (Gandhara): search Commons `Gandhara Buddha (tnm)`
  - Confucius: already in assets; also `Konfuzius-1770.jpg` on Commons
  - Jesus / Hillel / Muhammad: use with care (tradition-sensitive); quote-only is fine
  - Yoruba / Mahabharata: cultural art optional; quotes stand alone

## Theories

- One label + one line. You speak the critique.
- Relativism issue slide: abolition / suffrage / cruel punishment examples are **spoken**, not listed on slide.

## Fiduciary duties

- Seven name-only slides — you fill meaning.
- Loyalty, Care, Good Faith, Confidentiality, Disclosure, Records, Inform Clients.
- Punch line: money-power vs force-power standards.

## Wick

- Image, then question: client or hired force?
- Ties to citizens / officials and delegated state violence.

## Discussion block (HCC)

- Personal standards; balance of rules vs consequences.
- When is violence justified?
- Collect; do not grade "correct" ethics.
