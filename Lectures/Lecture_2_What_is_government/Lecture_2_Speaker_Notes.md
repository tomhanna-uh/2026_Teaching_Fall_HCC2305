## Opening (Why This Matters / Core Question)

- The empowerment promise is the most important slide of the day. Pause after it.
- Students should leave knowing *why* the definition of the state matters, not just the definition itself.
- Transition: “Everything we do for the rest of the semester rests on this distinction.”

## Before You Walked Onto Campus

- Genuine discussion prompt. Give 60–90 seconds of silence if needed.
- Typical answers: traffic laws, financial aid, bus/rail, campus police, tuition, food safety, etc.
- Collect 4–5 answers on the board or verbally, then say: “All of those are politics.”

## A Word on Words (three short slides)

**Government**  
- Emphasize the two meanings so students stop treating “the government” as only the current office-holders.

**State**  
- The Political Science meaning is the one we will use for the rest of the course.  
- Quick check: “When I say ‘the state’ from now on, what do I mean?”

**Politics**  
- Short definition only. The rest of the lecture fills it in.

## What Do Most People Think Politics Is?

- Read the list slowly. Ask for a show of hands on “distant” and “expensive.”
- This sets up the contrast with the Houston images.

## Houston Images Sequence

- Public Works → Metro → HCC campus.
- Point out that every student in the room is standing inside a political decision (the existence and funding of this campus).
- Ask: “Which of these felt ‘distant’ this morning?”

## What Politics Actually Is

- Contrast list. Keep it brisk.
- Key line: “It involves all of us.”

## Politics Is Powerful (build + images + empty “Power” slide)

- The incremental build is deliberate. Do not rush the second bullet.
- After the question “Why does politics have such power to cause harm?” — pause. Let them sit with it.
- Washington and Douglass images: give students a moment to read the quotes themselves before you comment.
- The nearly empty “Power” slide is intentional. Stand in silence for 3–4 seconds. Then connect back to the opening promise.

## Collective Action

- Simple definition. Most students already understand the idea; the point is the label.

## What Makes Government Different?

- This is the hinge of the whole lecture.  
- Do not answer it yourself yet. Move straight into the Other Organizations sequence.

## Other Organizations (Group 1 then Group 2)

- Reveal one organization at a time.
- After each addition, ask the two questions (common / different) only if energy is high; otherwise save the questions for the end of the sequence.
- Group 1 = voluntary / mostly legitimate in students’ eyes.  
- Group 2 = also use force, but we do not grant them legitimacy.

**Key Questions slide**  
- Group 1 enforcement: social pressure, expulsion, refusal to deal, etc. — almost never violence.  
- Group 2: they use force but lack legitimacy; we call it crime or terrorism.

## What Is “the State”?

- Read the definition slowly, word by word.
- “Organized” — not random individuals.  
- “Coercive” — you cannot simply opt out.  
- “Violent force” — ultimately backed by guns, courts, prisons.  
- “Legitimate” — most people accept it as rightful (this is the crucial Weberian piece).
- This is the payoff for the entire “Other Organizations” sequence.

## What Is Politics? (final definition)

- Now the earlier short definition is complete.
- Politics = collective decisions inside the coercive sphere.

## Two Spheres / Politics Is About

- Voluntary vs Coercive is the conceptual takeaway students should be able to repeat.
- If time is short, this is the slide to protect.
