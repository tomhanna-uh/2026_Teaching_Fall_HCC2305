# Lecture 8 Speaker Notes — Bill of Rights Part 1 (HCC)

**Date:** September 14, 2026  
**Course:** GOVT 2305 Federal Government

---

## Empowerment

Students should leave able to: (1) explain that "Congress shall make no law" originally bound only the federal government until incorporation in the 1940s+, (2) show how the religion clauses barred a *national* establishment while protecting free exercise and leaving state establishments intact, (3) see the home as a repeated special zone (3rd, 4th, property side of 5th), and (4) use the absence of a general privacy right as a basis for asking "What is missing?"

---

## Federal-only scope

Emphasize: as understood at the Founding and through Barron v. Baltimore (1833). Changes begin in the 1940s via 14th Amendment incorporation. Preview only — full treatment in Court Cases.

Speech / press / assembly / petition: you will mention verbally that they return later; nothing on slides.

---

## Religion

- No national church, no birth membership, no national tax support, no federal clergy salaries.
- 1776: all 13 had establishments; VA 1786, NH 1817, MA 1833.
- Establishment and free exercise work *with* each other.
- "Separation of church and state" is not in the text — open the policy question without resolving it.

---

## Second Amendment

As written: militia explanation + right of the people. Disagreement about the explanation does not erase the right. Keep thin; no deep modern case law this day.

---

## Fifth — property only this day

Due process of life, liberty, **property** + Takings (public use + just compensation).  
Criminal-suspect pieces of 5th, 6th, 8th → later Court Cases lectures. One explicit "Later" slide so students know the deferral is intentional.

---

## Ninth / Tenth

Rights retained by the people; powers reserved to the states or the people.  
`rights_powers.jfif` + "person vs citizen" beat. Supports the larger theme that the Founders distinguished categories carefully.

---

## Privacy discussion

No general right to privacy in the text; massive home protections nonetheless.  
Discussion: Should we pass a right-to-privacy amendment? What should it say? How and where should it apply?  
Let students propose language; you can note trade-offs (breadth vs. justiciability) without forcing a conclusion.
